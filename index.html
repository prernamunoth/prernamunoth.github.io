How it started

I built a Canvas Rubric & Quiz Builder to cut the hours faculty and designers spent rebuilding rubrics and quizzes by hand. A quiz with 50+ questions took 5–6 hours to build, and rubrics often lived in Word documents or only as assignment descriptions. The tools cut build time by 85% (from 5–6 hours to 30 minutes) across 25+ courses and won first place at the 2026 SMU Technology Showcase.

Where it went next

Rubrics and quizzes were only one part of the job. Designers were still moving between documents, spreadsheets and separate tools to write outcomes, align assessments and prepare for Quality Matters review. So I brought everything into one place. Course Design Studio now takes a course from source material to Canvas-ready content in a single workflow:

Outcomes

Writes course and module outcomes from source material, then checks each one for verb, Bloom level, measurability and fit.

Assessments and assignments

Suggests activities and assignments aligned to each outcome.

Alignment, grading and QM

Builds the alignment map, grading policy, course blueprint and Quality Matters plan.

Rubric App

Turns assignments or Word rubrics into Canvas-ready rubrics.

QTI Converter

Turns quiz documents into Canvas-importable quiz packages.

Alt Text Generator

Drafts image descriptions for accessibility.

Every tool has a review step: nothing goes into a course until a person has checked it.

Design decisions

Why one workflow instead of separate tools

Once the rubric and quiz tools were in use, I could see where the rest of the time went. Designers re-entered the same course details in different tools and kept outcomes, assessments and QM notes in separate documents, which made alignment hard to check. In one studio, the course is set up once and each step builds on the last: outcomes flow straight into assessments, alignment and the QM plan.

Why a review step before anything is exported

AI drafts quickly, but faculty are responsible for what students see. Every tool shows a preview first: outcomes are checked for Bloom level and measurability, rubrics are validated before export, and quizzes are flagged for review. The AI does the slow work, and people make the decisions.

Why Streamlit plus custom GPTs

Custom GPTs let faculty start right away in a tool they already knew, with no setup. But GPTs can't reliably produce files Canvas will import. Streamlit let me build that part: structured outputs, previews and direct Canvas downloads. Using both meant faculty got help immediately while I built the reliable version.

Fitting how faculty actually work

Faculty start from different places, so the tools meet them there. Rubrics can be generated from an assignment, imported from Word or CSV, built by hand or started from a template, with optional range scoring. Quizzes can come from Word, PDF or text files, with or without an answer key. Step-by-step guides cover the rest, including how to move quizzes into New Quizzes.

Results

  • Rubric & Quiz Builder: build time cut by 85% across 25+ courses, tracked in a log.
  • 200 rubrics for 50 faculty: 160 created from assignment details and 40 converted from rubrics in documents.
  • 100 quizzes for 25 faculty.
  • First place at the 2026 SMU Technology Showcase.

What's next

Taking the studio online so more faculty and designers can use it on their own.
