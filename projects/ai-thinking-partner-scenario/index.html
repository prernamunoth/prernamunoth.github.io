AI as a Thinking Partner: Getting Help Without Giving Up Your Thinking

A branching scenario in which the learner, a strategy manager at a fictional company, advises the COO on whether to expand an AI pilot, weighing productivity gains, data risks and employee feedback.

Problem

Knowing the principles isn't the same as applying them under pressure. Learners needed to practise a realistic, time-pressured decision where using AI well means balancing early wins against risk.

Audience

Professionals who use AI at work. Designed as practice alongside Pick the Role Before You Prompt.

My role

I wrote the scenario, choices, feedback and scoring, and built it in Articulate Rise.

Design decisions

  • Learners play a strategy manager advising the COO, so the stakes are clear: the recommendation affects the whole company.
  • Four decisions, each tied to one learning goal, such as weighing incomplete evidence or deciding what AI can safely handle.
  • Each choice earns full, partial or no credit, because real decisions are rarely simply right or wrong, and the character's reply changes with each choice so learners see the effect.
  • The answer order shuffles on every attempt, so learners can't pass by remembering positions.
  • The results page shows each goal as passed, partly passed or missed, so learners know exactly what to work on.
  • Next version: branch the conversation based on earlier choices.

Results

A score of 80% or higher passes, and a one-question survey asks whether learners feel more equipped to work with AI.
