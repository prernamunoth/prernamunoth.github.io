Disputes onboarding · 25 minutes

Fraud or a merchant problem? Get the dispute right the first time.

Many members say "I didn't make this charge" when they don't recognize a merchant name. If you file those calls as fraud, members lose their card and wait longer, and the case often has to be redone. Others really are fraud and need fast action, and some callers arrive angry. This module gives you practice with all three.

  • 1

    Verify a member and find a disputed charge in the agent console.

  • 2

    Choose the dispute reason that matches what happened, separating fraud from a canceled recurring charge.

  • 3

    Protect the account quickly when a charge is real fraud, and stay calm with a frustrated member.

  • 4

    Set honest expectations and close the call so the member doesn't need to call back.

Design notes for reviewers

Audience and goal

New and tenured card-disputes agents at a consumer fintech. The business problems: calls filed with the wrong dispute reason, slow action on real fraud and escalations from frustrated members, which cause rework, losses, unnecessary card replacements and repeat contacts.

Approach

Show, try, do. A guided system simulation builds the clicks; practice mode removes the scaffolding; three branching calls put the judgment under conversational pressure, contrasting a merchant dispute, real fraud and a frustrated member so agents learn to tell them apart. Feedback names the consequence for the member and the metric, not just right or wrong. Hard mode removes the options: learners type replies, Claude plays the member and scores each reply against a rubric, and coaching is held back until the debrief. Without Claude, learners self-check against the same rubric and a model answer.

Evaluation plan

  • Level 1: a two-question pulse after the module.
  • Level 2: simulation accuracy and call score, captured per learner.
  • Level 3: QA audits of dispute reason accuracy for 30 days after training.
  • Level 4: reason-code rework rate, repeat contacts within 7 days and CSAT on dispute calls, compared with a pre-training baseline.

Accessibility

  • Every control works by keyboard, with visible focus.
  • Coach feedback and the transcript are announced to screen readers.
  • State is shown with text as well as color; motion respects reduced-motion settings.
  • Works on phone and desktop, in light and dark mode.

How it was built

Written as a single web page with Claude as an AI coding and drafting assistant. I wrote the learning objectives, scenario logic and feedback criteria, and reviewed every line of content. In a production build, the same design would be reproduced in Storyline or the company's authoring tool and checked with disputes SMEs.

Assumptions

Fernway, its console and its 10-business-day provisional credit policy are invented for practice. Real dispute timelines depend on federal regulations, card network rules and the institution's own policy, and would come from the compliance team.
