⚡ Sales Intelligence

Typical Deal Triggers

Most deals are triggered by one of four events: (1) A current LMS contract renewal is 6–18 months out; (2) A new superintendent or CTO was just hired; (3) The district received a grant (ESSER funds, Title I/IV) with a learning technology line item; (4) A recent state audit cited learning outcome deficiencies. Always ask: "What's driving your interest in evaluating a new platform right now?" — the answer shapes your entire pitch.

Module 2 · Buyer Intelligence

Know Your Buyer Personas

EdTech deals involve 3–5 stakeholders and take 60–180 days to close. Know who's in the room, what they care about, and how to speak their language.

🏫
The Superintendent
District Superintendent / Deputy Supt.
Worried about board accountability, state assessment scores, equity gaps, and budget scrutiny. Rarely in the weeds — speaks in outcomes, not features.
Lead with: Outcome data, peer district wins, board presentation materials
💻
The CTO / IT Director
Chief Technology / Information Officer
Terrified of data breaches, FERPA violations, IT overhead, and failed rollouts. Has been burned before. Needs to see security certs, SLAs, and integration docs before budging.
Lead with: SOC 2 docs, Clever/ClassLink integration, zero-config rostering demo
📚
The Curriculum Director
Director of Curriculum & Instruction
Needs content to align to state standards, wants teacher adoption, and hates tools that add to teacher workload. If teachers don't use it, she'll kill the deal.
Lead with: Standards alignment maps, teacher time-savings data, PD Hub preview
💰
The CFO / Finance Director
Chief Financial Officer / Budget Office
Every dollar is scrutinized. They need to justify spend to the board, tie it to grant compliance, and know exactly what the 3-year total cost looks like.
Lead with: TCO calculator, grant eligibility (ESSER/E-Rate), 3-year ROI model
🗺️ Navigating the Buying Committee

The Champion Strategy

In 78% of won deals, reps identified a strong internal champion early — typically the Curriculum Director or an Instructional Technology Coordinator. Your champion doesn't have final authority, but they navigate internal politics, pre-sell stakeholders, and keep the deal alive between your touchpoints. Ask directly: "Who's most excited about solving this problem inside your district?" Then arm that person with executive summaries, ROI decks, and peer reference calls they can use without you in the room.

🗓️ Deal Cycle Reality

Timeline Benchmarks

Small district (<1,000 students): 45–75 days, 2–3 stakeholders

Mid-size district (1,000–10,000): 90–150 days, 4–6 stakeholders, school board vote likely

Large district (10,000+): 120–240 days, formal RFP process, legal review

Higher Ed institution: 90–180 days, faculty senate sign-off often required

Pro tip: Always ask in the discovery call — "Has your district purchased a new platform over $50K before? What did that process look like?"

🎯 Discovery Questions That Win

Ask These Every Call

1. "What tool are you using today, and when does that contract expire?"

2. "What's the single biggest learning outcome problem your district is trying to solve this year?"

3. "If a new platform worked perfectly, what would be different for teachers in 90 days?"

4. "Who else needs to be part of this conversation before a decision gets made?"

5. "Do you have any grant funding — ESSER, Title IV, E-Rate — that could apply here?"

Why This Happens
District budgets are genuinely tight, but "no budget" almost always means one of three things: (1) they haven't identified the right funding source, (2) the ROI hasn't been made tangible yet, or (3) you're talking to someone who doesn't control the budget. Clarify before you pitch a solution.
Script
"I completely understand — every district I talk to is dealing with competing budget priorities. Before we assume it's a dead end, can I ask a couple of questions? First, has your district received any ESSER III funds or applied for E-Rate Category 2 funding? A lot of our customers use those to fully cover their first year. And second, what's your current spend on the tools you're stitching together to do what LearnForge does in one place? We usually find the consolidation savings alone offset most of the investment."
Proof Points
73% of our new K–12 customers used ESSER or Title IV funding — we have a funding guide we can send your CFO
Avg district consolidates 3–5 tools when switching to LearnForge, saving $12–18 per student in redundant software costs
E-Rate Category 2 can cover up to 80% of platform costs for qualifying districts — our team has helped 140+ districts apply
Pitfall
Don't immediately offer a discount. It devalues the product and signals desperation. Lead with funding sources and ROI math first.

🔑 Key Lessons from This Deal

What Every Rep Should Take Away

1. Research triggers before outreach. Sarah found the contract expiry via public procurement records — not luck.

2. The stated problem is rarely the real problem. "Tech consolidation" masked the real pain: audit failure risk. Better discovery unlocked the real urgency.

3. Build champions, not just relationships. The Curriculum Director did half the selling internally. Sarah gave her the ammunition to do it.

4. Anticipate every stakeholder's concern before they voice it. The board data privacy one-pager was prepared before Day 1 because Sarah assumed it would come up.

5. TCO beats sticker price every time. When a CFO sees the full stack cost, the "expensive" vendor often wins on total value.
